The colony as a block of earth

What Hormigas could look like in 3D: your nest as a slab of soil cut open at the front, like an ant farm on a museum plinth. The chambers and tunnels are carved into the cut face, the surface sits on top, and you can turn the block to see it's solid. Play with it first; the proposal and the decisions I need are below.

1 · The short version

Five calls, one direction

World

An ant farm you can turn

A slab of soil cut open at the front. The front stays a readable cross-section, exactly like today, but with real depth, light and shadow. You turn it a little, never fly around inside.

Palette

Keep the soil, add daylight

The soil, amber ants and food colours stay. The world gets a sky that follows your clock, glowing chambers at night, and the menus get a light "field-guide paper" theme to match.

Interaction

Same verbs, more physical

Every system already has a place in the nest. In 3D, parties walk out across the surface, raids march in, diggers haul soil up the shaft. Menus open from the world; the server still decides.

Technology

Three.js beside the 2D nest

Add Three.js as a lazy-loaded scene on the Colony screen only. The PixiJS nest stays as the fallback for old phones, no WebGL and Motion off. No change to the server.

Art

Code first, a few hero models

Ants stay procedural (one body plan, legs animated in code, no rigging). The soil and chambers are built in code. Only a handful of hero props would be made as 3D models.

2 · The world

Two ways to add depth

A · Recommended

The specimen slab

The nest is a block of earth on a plinth, cut open at the front. Chambers are carved hollows you see into; tunnels are grooves; the surface on top carries parties, raids and events. A gentle orbit (about ±50°) shows it's solid without losing the cross-section.

  • Fits: it is literally an ant farm, the thing every player already pictures. The cross-section that makes the 2D nest readable is untouched.
  • Costs: a new renderer, 3D versions of the chamber art, and more care on cheap phones.
B · Low-change

2.5D: keep PixiJS, fake the depth

Stay in 2D and add depth with lighting: carved chamber rims, soft shadows, parallax soil layers, a glow at night. Ants and chambers stay as they are now.

  • Fits: almost no new code risk; every phone already runs it.
  • Costs: it will never feel like a place you can turn, and it's the look every other browser game has.

Not proposed: a free camera that flies through the tunnels. It loses the at-a-glance read of the whole colony, which is the core of a check-in game, and it is far more work. There is no rename map this time: the setting and names don't change.

4 · Type and icons

Keep the type, give resources a face

Display · Bricolage Grotesque The Granary is full Body · Atkinson Hyperlegible

Your foragers are 40 seconds from home with 120 foliage. A raid from Rojas arrives in 4 minutes.

Both are free on Google Fonts and already in the game. They stay: the display face has the hand-cut character the soil needs, and the body face is built for reading small numbers on phones.

Icon rules: side view, like the nest. A 3px ant-ink outline, one base colour and one shade, one highlight stroke. Always on a light tile with a leaf-green inner ring, so they read in both themes and on the 3D sky.

6 · Technology

Three.js next to what we have

Menus, sheets, HUDReact and HTML, unchanged
3D nest (new)Three.js scene, lazy-loaded on the Colony screen only
2D nest (kept)PixiJS: fallback for no WebGL, low-power phones and Motion off
Game state and APIThe same colony, missions and incoming data both nests read
ServerUnchanged: the server decides; the scene only shows it
OptionVerdict
Three.js, plainRecommended. The nest is one hand-driven scene, like nestScene and nestFx today; plain Three.js fits that shape. About 160 KB extra, only on the Colony screen.
React Three FiberGood library, wrong fit: it suits many small React-managed objects; ours is one animated scene.
Babylon.jsCapable but about three times heavier, with features we won't use.
PixiJS 2.5DOption B above. Cheapest, but no real depth.
Godot or Unity web exportNo: a second app, slow downloads, and it can't share the React menus.

Loading

Three.js plus the scene under 250 KB compressed, loaded only after the Colony screen opens; the 2D nest shows meanwhile. Hero models under 150 KB each, at most six.

Geometry

Under 60,000 triangles. Ants built from shared shapes, drawn in batches (at most 200 on screen). Flat colours; no textures beyond tiny painted strips.

Frame rate and battery

30 frames a second on a mid-range phone. Stop drawing when the tab is hidden or the nest is off screen. Sharper shadows only on desktop. Motion off shows a still frame or the 2D nest.

7 · Making the art

Code for most of it, models for a few

Now

Built in code

The slab, strata, chambers, tunnels, grass and every ant, as in this mock-up. Ants keep one body plan, the 3D twin of ants.ts, so castes, palettes and names carry over.

Hero pieces

A handful of models

The queen, fungus garden, honeypot ant, beetle and a fallen leaf: made from a concept image with an image-to-3D tool (Meshy, Tripo or Rodin), exported as .glb, reduced under 3,000 triangles each.

Later

An artist pass

If the game grows, hire a 3D artist for a consistent set and cosmetic nest themes, built on the same rules.

  1. Make the concept image

    Paste the prompt below into an image generator. Ask for a plain background. Keep the version you like.

  2. Turn it into a model

    Upload the image to Meshy or Tripo, choose "low poly" if offered, and download the result as .glb.

  3. Send it to me

    Drop the file in the chat. I check its size, reduce it if needed, match it to the palette and place it in the scene.

A honeypot ant hanging upside down, its abdomen swollen into a translucent amber sphere of honeydew. Stylised low-poly 3D model, flat-shaded facets, chunky readable silhouette, field-guide naturalism, no cartoon face. Colours: rust-brown body #A95F2F, amber #E8B23A with dark plate bands, warm rim light. Isolated on a plain light background, three-quarter side view, whole model in frame.

Check each tool's terms allow commercial use on the plan you use before anything ships in the game.

9 · Plan and decisions

Getting there without breaking the 2D nest

  1. Hidden test slice

    The 3D slab on the Colony screen behind a hidden switch, with your real chambers, tunnels and walking ants. You play it on your phone; I measure load time and frame rate.

  2. Port the moving parts

    Parties, raids, digging, brood and events in 3D; the chamber sheet and close-up open from the slab.

  3. Day, night and hero models

    Sky by your clock, glowing chambers at night, the first hero models.

  4. Make 3D the default

    The 2D nest stays for old phones and Motion off.

What I need from you

  1. Build the hidden 3D test slice (step 1), or stay 2D and do option B?
  2. Gentle orbit as in this mock-up, or a fixed front view with depth only?
  3. Keep the 2D nest permanently as the fallback and the Motion-off view?
  4. Add the light "field-guide paper" theme to the menus, following each player's device?
  5. Code-only art for now, or start on the hero models (some tools are paid)?

Risks: every new nest feature now costs work in 3D and, while it exists, in 2D too; cheap phones are the real test; art is the slowest part. Menus stay as the fallback for everything.
