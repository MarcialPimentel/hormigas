1 · The short version

Five calls

Structure

A ladder of scales

Garden → Patch → Nest → Chamber, one camera, one zoom. Each rung is a game screen you already have, turned into a place.

Look

Sunlit microcosm

Macro-photo light: low sun behind things, glossy backlit ants, colourful soil, flowers overhead. No more one-brown nest.

Swarms

Real crowds

Ants are 3D and instanced, so a raid is hundreds of ants and a swarm is a red tide of thousands, not 30 sprites.

Technology

More Three.js, no engine swap

Instancing and small shaders on the Three.js we already ship. No physics engine yet; the few physical moments are hand-written.

Art

One master ant, built in Blender

A low-poly ant and a garden kit made once in Blender. The 2D pictures get rendered from the same models, so menus and world match.

2 · The big idea

OGame had a solar system. We have a garden.

Space games feel big because you can zoom from a single ship out to a galaxy. Ants have the same ladder built in, and our world already uses it: the five regions are parts of one back garden, a patch is a square of ground, and the nest is under a mound.

Recommended

A. The scale ladder

Garden, Patch and Nest in 3D, joined by one zoom (scroll out past the edge and you rise a level). Colony screen = Nest. Missions screen = Patch. World = Garden. Lists stay as cards over the world.

Cost: the patch and garden are new scenes. The payoff: every screen you visit is a place.

B. Two separate 3D views

3D nest (have it) and a 3D patch on the Missions screen, with no zoom between them. The world grid stays 2D.

About 60% of the work, most of the wow, but the "one world" feeling is gone.

C. Hero moments only

Keep the screens as they are and add short 3D scenes for big events: a swarm arriving, a battle replay, a nuptial flight.

Cheapest. It looks great in a trailer, but day to day you'd still be using a menu.

Not proposed: a free camera you fly through the tunnels. It loses the at-a-glance read of the colony, and it turns a check-in game into a navigation game.

3 · Small things, big feeling

Eight rules borrowed from macro photography

Nature documentaries make an ant feel huge with a handful of lens and light tricks. These become the rules for every scale.

Get the camera down

Zooming in also lowers the camera, until it sits at ant height looking across the ground. The horizon rises and the grass becomes a forest.

Neighbours at true size

A marble, a bottle cap, a fallen leaf, a garden hose, drawn at their real size. Nothing says "you are 5 mm long" better.

Shallow focus

Out-of-focus grass across the bottom of the lens and soft sun discs at the top. Strongest up close, gone when you pull out.

Light from behind

A low warm sun behind the scene: chitin glows at the edges, grass blades light up green, shadows run toward you.

Crowds at real count

One drawn ant per ant, up to a cap. When the cap bites, the tag says the true number.

Slow world, quick ants

Grass sways slowly, light drifts slowly; ants scurry fast with a real tripod gait. The contrast in speed sells the scale.

Show the scale

A live scale bar ("view ≈ 80 cm across · 160 ant lengths") on every rung, so zooming always teaches you where you are.

Colour comes from life

Petals, aphids, fungus, honey, iridescent beetles, rusty clay. Brown is one colour among many, never the whole picture.

4 · The swarm look

Rivers, fans and threads

Columns are rivers. Every mission is a column walking a scent trail in the owner's colour. Ants have their own speed, so they overtake, bunch and spread. From far away it reads as a flowing line; up close, as hundreds of individuals.

Army ants come as a fan. Real Eciton raids sweep forward as a wide front with a column trailing behind, flushing insects ahead of them. The mock-up does exactly that: a red fan that narrows into a column, springtails leaping away, dust where it meets your soldiers, and upside-down dead left behind for your workers to haul home.

Far away, a light. At garden scale a colony is a glow and a mission is a thread. Night turns the garden into a star map.

Honest numbers. Up to about 3,000 ants on screen on computers and 900 on phones. Above that, each drawn ant stands for several and the tag gives the real count. The server still decides who wins. The ants only act out what it returns, so this is choreography, not a simulation (the vision's non-goal holds).

Build on what ships

React menus and cardsUnchanged; float over whichever scale is open
One Three.js world with three scenes: Garden, Patch, NestNew: one renderer and camera, lazy-loaded per scale
Instanced ants and grass, small shaders, hand-written "physics"New: thousands of ants in a few draw calls

Same colony, missions, incoming and reports data → serverUnchanged
OptionVerdict
Three.js WebGL2 + InstancedMesh + custom shadersRecommended. Already in the game. The mock-up draws 2,000+ swarming ants with walking legs in two draw calls.
Three.js WebGPU renderer (compute shaders)Later. For 50,000-ant swarms with GPU flocking. It ships in the same package, so it's an upgrade, not a rewrite.
Hand-written physics (hops, falls, splashes, sway)Recommended now. About 100 lines, no download, same result every time.
cannon-es (≈120 KB)Maybe later, for bodies tumbling in a battle replay.
Rapier (WASM, ≈1.5 MB)No. A great engine, but nothing here needs collisions solved, and it's a heavy download.
Post-processing (real depth of field and bloom)Phase 4, computers only. The mock-up fakes both cheaply.
React Three Fiber, Babylon.js, Unity/Godot webNo. Same reasons as the slab: one hand-driven scene, heavier downloads, a second app.
BlenderYes, for the master ant, caste variants, the garden kit, and rendering 2D pictures from the models.

Download

Three.js is already loaded on the Colony screen. Scene code under 150 KB; models under 800 KB in total (meshopt-compressed), loaded per scale.

Geometry

One ant ≤ 600 triangles. ≤ 3,000 ants on computers, ≤ 900 on phones. Under 100 draw calls per scale. Hidden groups aren't drawn.

Frame rate and battery

60 fps on computers, 30 on phones. Stop drawing when hidden or off screen. Motion off: still frame or the 2D view.

9 · Making the art

Code now, Blender for the heroes

Now, in code

Everything in the mock-up: the ant body plan (same parts as ants.ts), grass, flowers, pebbles, the log, soil horizons, chamber contents.

Hero pieces, Blender

A master ant (worker, soldier, queen, army ant and major as variants), a baked walk, and a garden kit: leaves, petals, pebbles, twig, acorn, bottle cap, marble, termite cone, aphid.

Later

A 3D artist for a consistent set, and cosmetic nest themes (they're palette and prop swaps, so never pay-to-win).

The Blender pipeline

  1. Model the worker low-poly (≤ 600 triangles) to the body plan, legs as separate segments.
  2. Make the castes as shape keys: big head and jaws (soldier), long gaster (queen), slim with two waist nodes (army ant), sickle jaws (major).
  3. Rig six legs, animate one tripod walk loop, and bake it to a vertex animation texture, so thousands walk on the GPU like in the mock-up.
  4. Export .glb with meshopt; I wire it into the instancing code.
  5. Render each model side-on with an ink line to make the 2D pictures.

I can drive Blender directly through its connector, which timed out in this session. If you open Blender with the MCP add-on before a session, I'll build the master ant there. Or make a concept image and let an image-to-3D tool rough it out:

11 · Plan and decisions

Value early, stop safely

  1. Colour and real ants in the slab. Instanced 3D ants replace the sprite cards, plus the soil horizons and the sunlit surface. Small and visible; phones keep 2D.
  2. The 3D patch on the Missions screen (computers): diorama, trails, columns, scents, wild sites. The drawn 2D patch stays as the fallback.
  3. Swarm and battle moments: the fan front, fleeing insects, the clash at the mound, corpses to haul, a replay from the report.
  4. The Garden and the zoom that ties the rungs together; nuptial flights; supercolony threads. Then real depth of field and bloom on computers.
  5. Blender hero art, and the 2D pictures re-rendered from it. Phones get the 3D patch if phase 2 holds 30 fps on a mid-range phone.

What I need from you

  1. Structure: A (the scale ladder), B (two separate 3D views) or C (hero moments only)? I recommend A.
  2. Look: retire the brown strata for the sunlit microcosm palette (soil horizons, backlit ants, flowers overhead)?
  3. Ants: real 3D ants from one model, instanced, replacing the flat cards in 3D?
  4. Counts: draw armies and swarms at their real size (capped, with the true number on the tag) instead of 1 per 3, max 30?
  5. First build: colour and real ants in the slab (my pick), or go straight to the 3D patch?
  6. Allies: teal for supercolony lights and threads?
  7. Hero art: I build the master ant in Blender (you open Blender with the connector), or you start from a concept image in Meshy or Tripo?

Risks: every system costs a little 3D work on top of its 2D fallback; cheap phones are the real test; hero art is the slowest part. The menus remain the fallback for everything.
