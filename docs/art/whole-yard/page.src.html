The Whole Yard

One map, from the whole back garden down to a single ant and into the nest. Civilization's way of showing a world (hex tiles, borders, mist, cities that grow, banners) on top of the game we already have, with its clock and its rules unchanged.

The short version

Structure

One map, one zoom

Yard, Region, Patch, Mound and Nest become one continuous zoom on one screen. No more switching between Colony and Missions to see your world.

Look

Civ's map grammar

Patches are hex tiles far out. Scent borders mark whose ground it is, mist hides what you haven't seen, mounds grow as colonies grow, and banners name them.

Rules

Civ's look, OGame's clock

No turns and no unit micro. The colony still lives while you're away. "End turn" becomes a Needs you button that takes you to whatever is idle or under threat.

Technology

Same stack, one scene

Three.js on the version we ship. The Garden and Patch scenes merge into one map with level-of-detail rules. The nest stays its own scene, joined by a dive through the mound.

Art

A Blender kit

Mounds in three growth stages (made for this page in Blender), then landmarks and the master ant. All of it scripted, so it can be rebuilt and tuned.

What makes it read as a 4X

Hormigas already has all four Xs: scouting, more colonies through Nuptial Flight, chambers and foraging, raids. Civilization's advantage is that you see them on one map. Here is what we borrow, and what each piece costs.

My recommendation on rules: take Civilization's presentation, not its rules. Turns, settlers walking tile by tile and moving units by hand would break two pillars ("the colony lives while you're away" and "no unit micro") and turn a 5-minute check-in into a 40-minute session. The Whole Yard gives the look and feel of a 4X while every action stays one of today's server actions.

One zoom, five levels

Each level shows what helps at that distance and hides the rest. The ladder at the top of the mock-up says where you are; the scale bar says how much of the yard is in view.

Playtesting it before players see it

You asked for a way to play this without shipping it. There are three, and they stack.

Now

This mock-up

Made-up data, but the real feel of the zoom, the dive and the four Xs. Good for yes / no on the direction.

RecommendedNext

A preview switch on the live game

The new map ships inside the game but stays switched off. You turn it on for your own browser with a link (?preview=yard) and play it with your real colonies. Players see nothing new. Switch it off and the game is exactly as it was.

Optional

A second server

A copy of the game at its own address with test players. Costs about one more small server a month and its own world; worth it only once several people test at the same time.

Loading

Under 1.5 MB more for the map code and models, loaded only on computers when the map opens. Regions far away load as plain tiles and fill in as you come closer.

Geometry

500 tiles in one draw call; grass, mist, ants and loads instanced. About 400k triangles at the busiest view; the mounds are 440–1,450 each.

Frame rate

60 fps on a mid laptop; 30 fps and a smaller shadow map on phones; the map stops drawing when hidden.

Making the art in Blender

The mounds in the mock-up were built for this page by a Blender script (docs/art/whole-yard/mounds.blend.py): dome, crater, pebbles, twig thatch, leaf bits, coloured faces. Change a number, run it again, get new mounds.

Plan

  1. Preview switch and the one zoom (computers): Garden and Patch merge into one map behind ?preview=yard; double-click your mound dives into today's 3D nest. Existing art.
  2. Civ grammar: hex tiles far out, scent borders, banners, the Needs you button.
  3. Mist: the server remembers which patches you've seen; scouting lifts it.
  4. Blender kit: growing mounds, landmarks, region dressing, the master ant.
  5. Make it the main screen for everyone (your call after playtests), then phones.

What I need from you

  1. Direction: go with the Whole Yard (Civilization's look, today's rules and clock)?
  2. Hex tiles: grid far out only, fading up close (recommended), or hexes at every zoom?
  3. Mist: add fog of war over patches you've never seen (needs a small server change)?
  4. Scent borders: art only for now (recommended), or should owning ground also give a bonus later?
  5. Playtest path: the preview switch on the live game, for your browser only (recommended)?
  6. Phones: computers first, phones keep today's screens until step 5 (recommended)?
  7. Tone: update the vision's "no cartoon faces" to "stylised field guide" to match the ants you just shipped?

Risks: a one-map screen is the biggest client change so far, so each step is its own PR and the old screens stay until you switch. 3D work costs more per feature than menus; the art kit is the slowest part.
